# Creative Philosophy

Use this discipline for visual identity, color, type, spacing, composition, motion, and the product's character.

Begin with the job and context, not a fashionable style recipe. Understand the user, main outcome, information and trust needs, and approved design choices. Define a small Product Design DNA that describes what the product should feel like and how that feeling appears in its interface. Separate approved decisions, observed evidence, and inferred hypotheses.

## Make taste contextual

- Preserve distinctive approved identity and strengthen what makes the product recognizable.
- Use hierarchy, alignment, spacing, color, typography, shape, imagery, iconography, and motion to serve the task and the product's character.
- Use restraint when it improves focus, but do not treat monochrome, minimalism, few borders, short copy, or stillness as universal goals.
- Keep personality when it is useful and approved. Richness, color, content density, and decoration can be right for one product and wrong for another.
- Reduce an element only after checking its task, meaning, trust, accessibility, state, policy, narrative, and identity role.
- Use one or two recurring signature cues where that suits the product; do not flatten intentionally expressive, media-rich products into a generic template.
- Give motion a job such as feedback, orientation, state, hierarchy, or approved delight. Honor reduced-motion preferences and never let animation imply unconfirmed success.
- Preserve an existing strong system rather than inventing a new palette, type scale, or component language without a reason.

## Turn adjectives into choices

Translate requests like “premium,” “modern,” “clean,” or “friendly” into specific changes tied to hierarchy, legibility, task flow, and approved DNA. When a material choice remains ambiguous, describe the tradeoff in plain language and offer a contextual recommendation. Do not ask users to configure tokens or typography vocabulary unless they ask for that detail.

Balance clarity, product identity, accessibility, and behavioral truth. If the best proposed change has no supported benefit or weakens the product, return **NO_CHANGE**.
